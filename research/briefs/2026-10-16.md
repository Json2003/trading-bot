## New evidence

### First-Passage Mechanics: When Passive Quotes Are Reached in Bitcoin Prediction Markets — No candidate

Published: 2026-10-13  
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7602563

The study examines 12,777 five-minute Bitcoin prediction markets. A hypothetical
bid placed at the displayed best bid four minutes before expiry was first seen
as reached within 60 seconds in 83% of markets. Mean observed penetration beyond
the quote was 2.335 cents, while a directionless first-passage null reproduced
2.279 cents. Only 0.056 cents remained after subtracting the null. The estimates
describe polled book states, not executions.

The hypothesis-design lesson is that a passive-order markout measured from the
first sampled touch is mechanically conditioned on the book having moved through
the quote. Raw markouts can therefore manufacture apparent adverse selection or
continuation. Any future maker or queue model must compare empirical markouts
with a frozen first-passage/null process and retain exchange event time,
collector receipt time, quote sequence, and actual fill status.

This is not a new market feature. The venue, contract payoff, and expiry
mechanics do not transfer directly to BTC/ETH perpetual futures, and the paper
does not observe fills.

### When Do Liquidity Voids Predict Reversion? Evidence from Cryptocurrency Order Books — No separate candidate

Posted: 2026-10-01  
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7546180

Using one month each of ETHUSDT and BTCUSDT spot order-book data, the study
reconstructs 2.85 million snapshots and identifies 745,000 liquidity-withdrawal
events. Mean reversion is positive after detected voids and statistically
significant at several horizons through ten seconds. The effect is monotone in
the depleted side's pre-event share of total depth: a withdrawal from the
already-thin side reverts more strongly, while withdrawal from the thick side
shows little or no effect. The authors do not find the effect tradeable as a
standalone strategy or useful for implementation-shortfall gating.

This is a useful conditioning rule for the existing book-reliability/execution-
fragility component, but not a separate signal. A future preregistered
falsification split should distinguish thin-side from thick-side withdrawals
using point-in-time L2 data. The result fails for this bot if it cannot improve
the baseline after next-open latency, realistic fills and funding, and the
shared 86-bps stress cost. The one-month-per-asset sample and spot-to-perpetual
transfer are material limitations.

No JSON is added because a separate liquidity-void candidate would overlap the
existing book-reliability component, and the paper reports no standalone
tradability.

### Touch Must Dominate Digital: A Model-Free Bound Violated on Executable Quotes in a Permissionless Derivatives Market — No candidate

Posted: 2026-10-06; revised: 2026-10-08  
Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7563559

Across 46,312 one-minute observations for paired Hyperliquid outcome contracts,
a model-free pricing bound was violated at 48.2% of mid-price timestamps and
25.0% of executable-quote timestamps. The violations clustered in episodes
lasting as long as 38 hours; different makers often set the two legs and 94% of
orders were cancelled rather than filled. Gross distinct-quote opportunities
were estimated at 3,822 USDC before fees.

The result reinforces a cross-venue design rule: algebraic or relative-price
inconsistency is not a tradeable signal unless both legs are simultaneously
executable with point-in-time depth, queue position, latency, fees, and
cancellation risk. It does not justify a BTC/ETH perpetual feature because the
instruments are permissionless outcome contracts, the data are commercial, and
the reported amount is pre-fee rather than realized profit.

## Overall-indicator decision

No component is added to the Microstructure State indicator this week. The
existing five-component research design remains unchanged:

1. funding persistence/fragility;
2. cross-venue liquidity dispersion;
3. flow compression;
4. book reliability/execution fragility;
5. cross-venue flow.

The new evidence changes evaluation requirements only:

- passive-order markouts require a frozen first-passage null;
- liquidity-withdrawal tests require a preregistered thin-side/thick-side split;
- cross-instrument or cross-venue discrepancies require simultaneous executable
  quotes and realistic multi-leg fills.

No candidate JSON was added. Active inference tags, trade gates, orders,
leverage, risk controls, model promotion, and indicator weights remain
unchanged.
