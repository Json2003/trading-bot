## New evidence

### Order-flow toxicity in cryptocurrency perpetual futures — No, not yet

Published/materially revised: 2026-10-05  
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iref.2026.105876

The study uses roughly 62.16 million second-level Bybit BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT
observations over 360 out-of-sample days. Its TailScore combines a predicted
toxic-event probability with a predicted 99th-percentile five-second absolute
move. The fixed 1% gate produced an illustrative avoided-loss estimate, but
class weighting means raw probabilities are not calibrated event probabilities;
simpler return/flow/volatility models sometimes ranked tail risk more
efficiently, and matched events did not show a stronger price-impact
persistence advantage.

This is relevant to the aggregate indicator as a possible execution-fragility
component, not a directional feature. It is not testable yet because the full
19-feature schema, preprocessing specification, and model hashes were not
independently verified. No candidate JSON was added. Before nomination, recover
the replication archive, verify prior-second alignment, reproduce the published
walk-forward result, and then collect real `timestamp,available_at,value`
records forward in time.

### Mixture-of-Experts for Cryptocurrency Order Execution — No new signal

Published: 2026-10-02  
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03369

On Binance BTC/USDT order-book data, no tested mixture-of-experts configuration
significantly improved mean implementation shortfall over vanilla DDQL across
100 seeds. Learned policies underperformed TWAP and immediate liquidation in
the replay environment. Annealed exploration removed observed policy collapse,
while larger expert counts increased within-policy tail risk.

The result changes evaluation requirements rather than adding a market
component: future execution research must report multiple seeds, fixed TWAP and
immediate-execution baselines, collapse frequency, tail shortfall, and realistic
latency/queue/fill assumptions. No candidate JSON was added.

### Measuring Trade Direction in a Prediction Market — No candidate

Published: 2026-10-05  
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06412

Across 24.5 million Polymarket prints matched to on-chain settlement, trade-sign
methods differed materially: balanced accuracy was 0.942 for Lee–Ready, 0.768
for the tick test, and 0.670 for retrospective bulk-volume classification.
Measured price-impact signs could reverse under some weighting choices.

This does not transfer directly to perpetual futures, but it reinforces a bot
data rule: preserve exchange-native maker/taker fields and raw messages; do not
silently replace native aggressor-side data with inferred signs. No candidate
JSON was added.

## Aggregate indicator integration

The research branch now contains `research/microstructure_state.py`. It accepts
only measured component records with explicit observation and availability
timestamps, rejects stale or non-normalized inputs, and returns `unknown` until
at least three of five frozen components are fresh:

1. funding persistence/fragility;
2. cross-venue liquidity dispersion;
3. flow compression;
4. book reliability/execution fragility;
5. cross-venue flow.

The score is a fixed equal-weight mean of normalized component values in
`[0, 1]`. It is marked `research_only` and is not connected to live orders,
inference tags, trade gates, leverage, risk, or promotion. Missing/stale values
are not converted to zero.

Any future validation must use point-in-time measurements, next-open latency,
realistic fills and funding, at least 20 independent observations per
walk-forward block where applicable, the shared 86-bps stress cost, and
untouched chronological confirmation. Thresholds, weights, assets, and horizons
must be frozen before screening.
